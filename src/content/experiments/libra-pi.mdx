## Overview

LIBRA-Pi is the step between the litre-scale [BABY 1 L](/experiments/baby-1l) experiments and
[LIBRA-One](/experiments/libra-one). In the team's words, "the Pi tank is designed to
cover π steradians around the point neutron source, while also resembling a
slice of pie". It is a full-scale sector of the original four-quadrant LIBRA
design, with a salt thickness of several 14 MeV neutron mean free paths.

LIBRA-Pi uses ClLiF, the beryllium-free surrogate salt used in BABY, to
study what only appears at scale:

- how far neutrons travel through the salt (neutron path length);
- handling a large volume of salt;
- heating and thermal management;
- neutron multiplication.

## Experimental setup

The tank is built from Inconel 625 with a double wall, so that tritium
permeating through the inner wall can be measured, and a plasma-sprayed
alumina coating on the outside to limit further permeation losses. Heating
the salt to its melting point and on to 700 °C takes several days, and
structural analysis shows the vessel stays within allowable stresses.

floor area of about 1,230 cm².

### Heating

The salt is heated from inside and outside.

- **Cartridge heaters.** Three re-entrant tubes, 5 cm (2 in) across, drop
  through the lid to about 7.6 cm above the floor of the tank. Each holds a 1 in
  cartridge heater rated at 5 kW (240 V), with an Incoloy 800 sheath and a
  type K thermocouple at the middle of its heated length. The heated length
  (about 47 cm) was chosen so that it stays fully under the salt surface
  with a 70 L fill, which reaches about 52 cm above the bottom of the
  tubes. The rest of each heater (about 84 cm) is unheated, so its leads
  sit well above the hot zone.
- **Heater jacket.** A shaped jacket wraps the outside of the tank: two
  zones of about 2.4 kW each (120 V, 4.8 kW in all), with a nominal
  temperature of 500 °C and its own thermocouple per zone, faced in PTFE
  over fibreglass insulation.
