## Overview

LIBRA-Pi is the step between the litre-scale [BABY 1 L](/experiments/baby-1l)
experiments and [LIBRA-One](/experiments/libra-one), with about 100 L of salt.
The tank is a 90° sector of an annulus around the neutron source. <mark>In the
team's words, "the Pi tank is designed to cover π steradians around the point
neutron source, while also resembling a slice of pie".</mark> In the team's
neutronics models, four LIBRA-Pi tanks around one source make up the full
LIBRA experiment.

The salt is 28 cm thick<mark>, several mean free paths of a 14 MeV neutron</mark>. LIBRA-Pi
uses ClLiF, the beryllium-free surrogate salt used in BABY, to study what only
appears at scale:

- <mark>how far neutrons travel through the salt (neutron path length);</mark>
- <mark>handling a large volume of salt;</mark>
- <mark>heating and thermal management;</mark>
- <mark>neutron multiplication.</mark>

Its larger gas system should allow flow rates of about 400 sccm, against
30 to 50 sccm in BABY 1 L, enough to stir the salt and move tritium release
out of the diffusion-limited regime seen at 1 L.

## Experimental setup

The tank is built from Inconel 625 with a double wall, so that tritium
permeating through the inner wall can be measured<mark>, and a plasma-sprayed
alumina coating on the outside to limit further permeation losses. Heating
the salt to its melting point and on to 700 °C takes several days, and
structural analysis shows the vessel stays within allowable stresses.</mark>

### Heating

The salt is heated from inside and outside. The neutronics model includes
three re-entrant heater tubes through the lid.

- <mark>**Cartridge heaters.** The three re-entrant tubes, 5 cm (2 in) across,
  drop through the lid to about 7.6 cm above the floor of the tank. Each holds
  a 1 in cartridge heater rated at 5 kW (240 V), with an Incoloy 800 sheath
  and a type K thermocouple at the middle of its heated length. The heated
  length (about 47 cm) was chosen so that it stays fully under the salt
  surface with a 70 L fill, which reaches about 52 cm above the bottom of the
  tubes. The rest of each heater (about 84 cm) is unheated, so its leads sit
  well above the hot zone.</mark>
- <mark>**Heater jacket.** A shaped jacket wraps the outside of the tank: two
  zones of about 2.4 kW each (120 V, 4.8 kW in all), with a nominal
  temperature of 500 °C and its own thermocouple per zone, faced in PTFE
  over fibreglass insulation.</mark>
