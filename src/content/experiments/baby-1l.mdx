## Overview

BABY 1 L is the second stage of BABY (Build A Better Yield), after
[BABY 100 mL](/experiments/baby-100ml). It scales the breeder volume up
tenfold, with better thermal and gas handling, sparging and real-time neutron
diagnostics. The crucible sits inside an outer vessel, so that tritium
permeating through the crucible walls is collected as well as tritium
released into the gas above the salt.

The 1 L experiments have run since November 2024: twelve runs with ClLiF,
the beryllium-free surrogate salt used at 100 mL, until August 2025, then
FLiBe from November 2025. Runs with lead-lithium and lithium oxide are in
preparation. They lead to [LIBRA-Pi](/experiments/libra-pi) (about 100 L)
and then [LIBRA-One](/experiments/libra-one) <mark>(700 L)</mark>.

## Results

Across runs 1 to 4 the measured tritium breeding ratio (TBR) was
2.45 × 10⁻³, averaged over the runs weighted by neutron fluence. That is six
times the 100 mL value, largely because the larger volume of salt covers a
greater solid angle around the neutron source. The OpenMC model gives
2.08 × 10⁻³, in very good agreement.

Tritium came out of the salt more slowly than at 100 mL. The release is
limited by diffusion through the salt (a Sherwood number of about 1 in runs
1 to 3), between 630 °C and 750 °C under helium. Bubbling helium through the
salt with a simple sparger barely changed this.

Adding hydrogen to the helium changed it a great deal. With 1000 ppm H₂ from
the start (run 4), the run was over in four days, against 30 to 60 days with
pure helium, which fits an isotopic exchange mechanism. Tritium permeating
into the outer vessel stream was only detected once hydrogen was added. As at
100 mL, more than 96% of the tritium was collected in insoluble forms (HT or
T₂), whatever the gas, so the faster release is not explained by a change of
speciation alone. The results are published in
[Nuclear Fusion 66, 076046 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ae6f31).

The first two FLiBe runs irradiated the salt while solid. Their repositories
give measured TBRs of (3.33 ± 0.25) × 10⁻³ for run 1 and (2.48 ± 0.21) × 10⁻³
for run 2, not yet published. Run 3, on 22 July 2026, was the first
irradiation of molten FLiBe. The analysis for a
report on the first FLiBe experiment is in
[FLiBe-1L-paper](https://github.com/LIBRA-project/FLiBe-1L-paper).

Run 2 had a higher neutron fluence. Its heater failed mid-run and the salt
froze before it was re-melted, which may explain its slower release. The
high TBR in run 3 comes from a high tritium release after hydrogen was
added, likely including tritium held up from earlier runs. From run 4 on,
cyclotrons and other neutron sources in the MIT Vault lab may have
cross-irradiated the salt by an amount that could not be quantified. For
runs 9 to 11 the nGen-400 stood 1 m from the salt and 105 cm above the
floor, to test shielding; for run 11 the outer vessel was also wrapped in
alumina wool and fibreglass to raise the salt temperature.

### Breeder and containment

1.88 kg of ClLiF, the same 30.5 mol% LiF, 69.5 mol% LiCl eutectic as at
100 mL, made from 99.9% anhydrous LiCl (Apollo Scientific) and 99.99% LiF
(Thermo Scientific) with natural lithium, fills a cylindrical Inconel 625
crucible 14.6 cm across and 12.1 cm tall, with 3 mm walls. The crucible hangs
from its gas lines inside an outer vessel of 304L stainless steel, 27.3 cm
across and 21.1 cm tall, closed by a 2.54 cm thick conflat blank as the lid.
Ceramic insulation plates line the bottom of the outer vessel and cover the
crucible, and silica fabric lines its walls. The assembly sits on an epoxy
workbench lined with kaowool.

### Heating

A 1200 W cartridge heater (Watlow FIREROD, Incoloy 800 sheath) runs down the
vessel axis into a copper-lined re-entrant tube, to 0.9 cm above the bottom
of the crucible. Inside the outer vessel, a 1000 W radiative furnace
surrounds the crucible. Each has its own PID controller. At steady state the
heater reaches its 750 °C setpoint but the furnace reaches only 610 °C of its
700 °C, so the salt is at least 630 °C throughout: above the 510 °C melting
point of the eutectic, but below the 700 °C target used at 100 mL. An alarm
cuts the cartridge heater if the top of the workbench reaches 75 °C.

### Neutron source

The first runs used two commercial sealed-tube DT neutron generators, a
Thermo Fisher A-325 (runs 1, 3 and 4, about 0.8 to 0.95 × 10⁸ neutrons per
second) and a Thermo Fisher P-383 (run 2 and FLiBe run 1, about
2.6 to 2.9 × 10⁸). The generator lies under the workbench, its axis parallel
to the bench, with a gap of under 1.3 cm, cooled by a blower and a fan. Some
DD reactions add neutrons of about 2.45 MeV, which the activation foils do
not count. Irradiations with these generators took about 12 hours.

From run 6, in May 2025, the runs used an nGen-400 generator<mark>, made by
Starfire Industries</mark>. At 1.3 to 1.9 × 10⁹ neutrons per second it cut a
typical irradiation to 1 to 3 hours. Under the bench, its target plane is
5.5 cm below the epoxy plate, aligned with the vessel axis. Its logs are
analysed in
[nGen-log-analysis](https://github.com/LIBRA-project/nGen-log-analysis).

### Gas handling and tritium collection

The gas, ultra-high-purity helium or 1000 ppm H₂ in helium, passes oxygen
and moisture scrubbers and is split into two streams by mass flow
controllers:

- the **inner vessel stream** (30 sccm) passes through the crucible, either
  as cover gas over the salt or sparged through it from a tube lowered to
  about 1.3 cm above the bottom, and collects tritium released from the salt;
- the **outer vessel stream** (50 sccm) sweeps the outer vessel around the
  crucible and collects tritium that permeates the crucible wall.

Each stream passes sintered particulate filters, has 10 sccm of air mixed in,
and runs through its own bubbler: two water vials, which capture soluble
tritium (HTO, TF), a palladium-on-alumina oxidiser at 450 °C, which converts
insoluble forms (HT, T₂) to water, and two more vials. The system runs at
about 3 PSIG; while vials are sampled, the gas is diverted to vent. No
clogging from salt vapour or particles was seen.

Each sample of 10 mL of bubbler water and 10 mL of Ultima Gold LLT cocktail
is counted for 100 minutes on a PerkinElmer Tri-Carb 5110 TR. The minimum
detectable activity is under 0.1 Bq per sample.

## Diagnostics

- **Activation foils** of niobium and zirconium, about 5 cm above the neutron
  emission point on top of and below the generator tube, give the total
  neutron fluence for each run: ⁹³Nb(n,2n)⁹²ᵐNb (threshold 8.93 MeV,
  934 keV gamma) and ⁹⁰Zr(n,2n)⁸⁹Zr (threshold 12.1 MeV, 909 keV gamma).
  They are counted on two 10 cm NaI detectors in a lead shield. The foils are
  the largest source of uncertainty in the measured TBR.
- **The proton recoil telescope (PRT)** monitors the neutron emission rate in
  real time: a polyethylene converter foil in front of four diamond detectors
  in a telescope, counting coincidences to reject background. It confirmed
  the spectrum is dominated by 14 MeV DT neutrons.
- **Liquid scintillation counting (LSC)** of bubbler samples gives tritium
  release over time.

From FLiBe run 1 the foils are also counted on an HPGe detector.

## Modelling

The neutronics model is built in OpenMC with ENDF/B-VIII.0 nuclear data. It
includes the vessel assembly, the generator, its lead and polyethylene
supports, the workbench and, unlike the 100 mL model, the MIT Vault lab
itself, which scatters neutrons back into the salt. A 0D model of tritium
release, fitted to each run, gives the mass transport coefficients of the
inner and outer vessel release paths.

## Running an experiment

The outline of a BABY 1 L run, as presented by the team at SOFE 2025 and
recorded in the run repositories:

1. Fill the crucible with the breeder and hang it in the outer vessel.
2. Heat the salt with the cartridge heater and furnace.
3. Flow gas, cleaned of oxygen and moisture, through the inner and outer
   vessels.
4. Irradiate with 14 MeV neutrons, at 10⁸ to 10⁹ neutrons per second for 1
   to 12 hours depending on the generator.
5. Let the gas bubble through the water bubblers during and after
   irradiation, sampling the vials from time to time.
6. Count the samples by liquid scintillation.

## Data and analysis

Each experiment has its own version-controlled repository holding its data,
simulation, analysis and documentation, with the analysis run in Jupyter
notebooks and checked by continuous integration. The analysis uses
[libra-toolbox](https://libra-toolbox.readthedocs.io/en/latest/), the
team's open-source Python package for neutronics models, LSC data,
activation foils, PRT data and tritium transport models. The data behind the
paper is archived on Zenodo
([10.5281/zenodo.17201068](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17201068)).

Each run repository is created from a common template,
`libra-run-template`.
Raw data lives in `data/`: `general.json` for the run information, with LSC
data in `data/tritium_detection/` and neutron detection data in
`data/neutron_detection/`. Analysis lives in `analysis/neutron/` (the OpenMC
model and foil analysis) and `analysis/tritium/` (the tritium release model).
Each GitHub release creates a Zenodo record with a DOI, linked to the
[LIBRA-project community](https://zenodo.org/communities/libra-project/records). So far runs 1 to 4, FLiBe runs 1
and 2 and the two papers have releases.
