## Overview

BABY (Build A Better Yield) is the small-scale stage of LIBRA. A small volume
of molten salt is irradiated by 14 MeV neutrons from deuterium-tritium (DT)
neutron generators. Tritium bred in the salt is carried out by a helium sweep
gas, captured in water bubblers, and counted by liquid scintillation. The
measured tritium breeding ratio (TBR) is compared with a neutronics model,
giving experimental data to validate the models used to design breeding
blankets.

Working at small scale lets the team gain experience in neutron generation,
neutron detection and tritium accountancy, and refine the plans for LIBRA,
while keeping safety hazards low. The 100 mL experiments used ClLiF, a
mixture of lithium fluoride and lithium chloride, as a surrogate for FLiBe:
avoiding beryllium let the team build and troubleshoot the set-up before
adding the safety protocols and equipment that beryllium work needs.

The next stage is [BABY 1 L](/experiments/baby-1l), followed by
[LIBRA-Pi](/experiments/libra-pi) (about 100 L) and
[LIBRA-One](/experiments/libra-one) <mark>(700 L)</mark>.

## Results

The 100 mL experiments measured a TBR of 3.57 × 10⁻⁴ in run 5: 1.17 × 10¹⁰
tritons (21 Bq of tritium) collected for (3.35 ± 0.30) × 10¹³ neutrons over
24 hours of irradiation. Runs 4 and 6 gave 3.42 × 10⁻⁴ and 3.83 × 10⁻⁴. The
value is small because only about 2% of the generator neutrons reach the salt
(a solid angle of about 0.3 sr). At this scale lithium-7, not lithium-6,
breeds most of the tritium, since the neutrons in the crucible are fast.

The measured TBR was 2.3 times lower than the OpenMC model predicted. The
bubblers collected over 99% of the tritium in the gas, so the team's
hypothesis is that the missing tritium permeated through the crucible walls,
which the 1 L design tests with an outer vessel. (The later BABY 1 L paper
quotes 3.61 × 10⁻⁴ measured and 4.71 × 10⁻⁴ modelled for 100 mL.)

All the collected tritium was in insoluble forms (HT or T₂), not the soluble
TF expected from earlier work with FLiBe and FLiNaK under pure helium. The
team suggests the different salt chemistry as one explanation; removing the
hydrogen conditioning step did not change the result.

### Breeder and containment

190 g of ClLiF (the 30.5% LiF, 69.5% LiCl eutectic, with natural lithium)
fills a 1.6 mm thick Inconel 625 crucible, 101 mm tall and 42 mm across
inside. A re-entrant heater in the centre of the crucible keeps the salt at
700 °C, controlled from a sheathed thermocouple in the salt, and the crucible
is wrapped in alumina wool. There is no outer vessel: tritium that permeates
the crucible wall is released into the room.

### Neutron source

Two commercial sealed-tube DT neutron generators, a Thermo Fisher A-325 and
a Thermo Fisher P-383, sit one on each side of the crucible, as close to it
as they can be without exceeding their maximum operating temperatures. In
run 5, activation foils gave 1.16 × 10⁸ neutrons per second from the A-325
and 2.71 × 10⁸ from the P-383 (relative error 8.9%), over 24 hours of
irradiation in two periods. The spectrum is not purely 14 MeV: DD reactions
in the generators add neutrons of about 2 MeV.

### Gas handling and tritium collection

Before a run the system is conditioned with 3.5% H₂ in helium, then flushed
with pure helium, which sweeps across the top surface of the salt during the
run. The gas then passes two water vials, which capture the soluble forms of
tritium (HTO and TF), an oxidising furnace, which converts the insoluble forms
(HT and T₂) to HTO, and two more vials, which capture those. Each vial holds
10 mL of deionised water. Each vial collects about 90% of the tritium reaching
it, so each pair collects over 99%.

## Diagnostics

- **Niobium activation foils**, on top of each generator and aligned with its
  target plane (runs 4 to 6), give the neutron fluence. After a run they are
  counted between two 100 mm cubic NaI scintillation detectors, through the
  934.4 keV gamma ray from ⁹³Nb(n,2n)⁹²ᵐNb.
- **A diamond detector** (Cividec), about 35 cm from the centre of the
  crucible, records the neutron count rate in real time. Its efficiency was
  not yet measured, so the foils give the absolute neutron rate.
- **Liquid scintillation counting (LSC)** of the bubbler vials gives the
  tritium release over time.

## Running an experiment

The steps of a BABY 100 mL run, as described in the paper:

1. Fill the crucible with the salt.
2. Condition the system with 3.5% H₂ in helium for 4.5 hours at 30 ccm,
   then at least 1 hour of pure helium at 20 ccm.
3. Bring the salt to 700 °C; it takes a few hours to reach equilibrium.
4. Irradiate with 14 MeV neutrons (24 hours in two periods in run 5).
5. Change the bubbler vials every 12 hours until day 2, then regularly until
   no more tritium is collected.
6. Mix the water from each vial with 10 mL of Ultima Gold LLT scintillation
   cocktail and count it for 2 hours on a PerkinElmer Tri-Carb 5110 TR.

## Data and analysis

The data, analysis and post-processing scripts behind the Nuclear Fusion
paper are in
[insights-from-BABY-experiment-paper](https://github.com/LIBRA-project/insights-from-BABY-experiment-paper),
archived on Zenodo ([10.5281/zenodo.14455926](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14455926)).
The neutronics model uses OpenMC 0.14.0 with FENDL-3.1d cross sections.

Data for runs 3 to 8 is in
[BABY-100-mL-runs](https://github.com/LIBRA-project/BABY-100-mL-runs),
archived on Zenodo ([10.5281/zenodo.14538148](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14538148)).
Its notebooks use
[libra-toolbox](https://libra-toolbox.readthedocs.io/en/latest/), the shared
Python package for neutron detection and tritium analysis.
